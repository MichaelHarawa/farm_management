# Cobb broiler feed trial: formulation and nutrient review

Prepared 21 September 2026. Objective: growth and weight gain, without cost minimization.

**Status: calculated trial specification, conditional on ingredient and premix verification. It is not a laboratory certificate or proof of faster growth than commercial feed.** Have a poultry feed nutritionist or competent feed mill reconcile the ingredient certificates and premix label with this specification before manufacture, and check the first mixed sample before feeding. Do not substitute products by name alone.

Assume Cobb500 because the farm identified its birds as Cobb, without specifying the cross. Use a three-feed program: starter days 0–12, grower days 13–28, and final feed days 29–42. This adapts Cobb's medium/large-bird program: the last feed retains Grower 2 amino-acid and mineral levels, with energy sufficient for days 40–42. It is not an exact reproduction of Cobb's five-feed program. Later feeding requires reassessment.

## Why the earlier reference needs revision

The previous formula was scaled from Table 6 of the Malawi poultry training manual; it was not independently optimized. The following calculation uses one consistent INRAE-CIRAD-AFZ as-fed ingredient matrix. Its assumed crude protein values are maize 7.6%, extruded full-fat soya 36.0%, soybean meal 46.2%, and fishmeal 65.2%. These are reference averages, not results for the farm's ingredients. This differs from the earlier rough illustration using 8.5%, 40%, and 60%, and explains the changed protein estimates. Do not interpret the new estimates as evidence that the farm's actual feed had this composition.

## Shopping and quality specifications

- **Soybean meal:** obtain the certificate for the actual low-fat product. The matrix assumes 46.2% CP, 1.5% fat and the poultry digestibility values in the linked table. A 40–44% meal, expeller cake or soya bran cannot be substituted kilogram-for-kilogram.
- **Full-fat soya:** the matrix assumes extruded material at 36% CP and 18.4% fat. Require appropriate processing; raw, inadequately cooked and burnt soya are unsuitable substitutes. Have processing quality checked using appropriate protein-quality/trypsin-inhibitor tests, not appearance alone.
- **Fishmeal:** nominal 65% CP alone is insufficient. The matrix assumes calcium 4.13%, total P 2.64%, sodium 1.057% and chloride 1.64%. Obtain actual mineral/salt and freshness results. Salted dried fish, fish waste or material mixed with sand/bones requires a different calculation.
- **MCP:** the matrix assumes calcium 16.7%, total P 22.4% and available P 19.1%. Do not substitute DCP or mono-dicalcium phosphate at the same weight. The available-P number is a reference feeding value, not simply the total-P label.
- **Premix:** ask for a complete broiler vitamin/trace-mineral product at 3 kg/tonne, supplying the applicable Cobb supplementary vitamin and trace-element specification on printed page 10. Require a declared composition, dosing rate, expiry and suitability for the intended processing. This calculation assumes negligible macro-mineral contribution and no added amino acids or choline from its carrier. A limestone-based carrier, added salt/amino acids, a different dosing rate or a concentrate requires recalculation. Vitamins and trace minerals are not certified by putting 150 g of an unspecified product into a bag.
- **Amino acids/choline:** specify the products in the table. Lysine sulphate is not equivalent to lysine HCl; methionine analogues are not equivalent by weight to DL-methionine. A product labelled 60% choline chloride supplies about 44.8% choline. The listed addition supplies roughly 537 mg choline/kg feed; reconcile any choline already in the premix.
- **Oil and salts:** use fresh soybean oil, feed-grade limestone and sodium bicarbonate. Oil is weighed, not measured as the same number of litres. Do not use building lime or baking powder.

No phytase or other enzyme nutrient credit was assumed. If an enzyme-containing product is selected, the feed nutritionist should decide the corresponding mineral and energy adjustments. Do not independently subtract MCP based on an enzyme marketing claim.

The micronutrient product is the remaining specification dependency. Use the table to shop and request quotations, then have labels and certificates reconciled before mixing. Laboratory checks should include moisture, protein, fat, fibre, calcium, phosphorus and salt/minerals, plus ingredient-specific quality checks. Digestible amino acids and AMEn are calculated feeding values; a basic crude-protein test does not verify them.

## Manufacture and handling

Prefer a competent mill that can produce uniform starter crumble and suitable grower/final pellets. Ask whether its equipment can incorporate this oil level while maintaining pellet quality. A poorly mixed or excessively dusty feed can undermine a good nutrient specification. This formula does not establish that farm mash will match commercial pellets.

Use a calibrated gram scale for the small additions and a bulk scale for major ingredients. Preblend the small ingredients progressively into part of the maize allocated in the formula (for example, an initial carrier portion, then a larger portion) before incorporating them uniformly into the full batch. That maize is taken from the listed total, not added on top. Apply oil evenly during an appropriate mixer cycle. Mixing time must be established for that mixer; hand stirring is not proof of uniformity. A feed mill can test mixing homogeneity.

Label every batch with recipe, ingredient lots and date. Keep feed dry and cool; avoid long storage of the oil-rich mixture. Retain a sealed representative sample of each batch for investigation if growth disappoints.

## Farm trial

Suggested practical pilot: 120 chicks of the same hatch and source, randomly assigned across six comparable pens of 20 birds. Three pens receive the candidate program; three receive the usual CP or Proto program. Use the same commercial brand consistently as the comparator. Keep sex distribution, initial weight, stocking density, brooding, lighting, water, vaccination and veterinary disease/coccidiosis prevention comparable. If fewer pens are possible, treat results as less conclusive.

Before starting, record the buyer's target live weight and define success: reach it at least as quickly as the comparator, with comparable or better feed conversion and no deterioration in survival, leg health or flock uniformity. Do not judge only the largest birds.

Weigh all 20 birds in each pen at placement and weekly at the same time of day. Record feed offered, leftover feed, recoverable spillage, deaths/removals and their weights, and daily health observations. Analyze pen averages because birds sharing a feeder are not independent diet replicates.

- Average daily gain: change in average live weight divided by days, interpreted alongside removals.
- Mortality-adjusted FCR: feed consumed divided by (ending live biomass + biomass of deaths/removals at removal - starting live biomass).
- Also report ordinary saleable-output efficiency and mortality separately; adjusted FCR must not conceal losses.
- Record the day each pen reaches the agreed sale weight and the spread of individual weights.

As a practical pre-agreed investigation trigger, pause the experimental feed if weekly gain is more than 10% below the comparator; this is a proposed trial rule, not a biological standard. Reduced intake, lameness, unusual diarrhoea or increased deaths warrant immediate investigation rather than waiting for weighing day. Return to the established complete feed while the feed supplier/nutritionist and poultry veterinarian investigate. Repeat a promising result in a second flock before scaling up.

## Calculation method and evidence

For each nutrient: sum(ingredient weight × ingredient nutrient concentration) / total mixture weight. All composition values use the as-fed basis. Standardized ileal digestible poultry amino-acid values are used consistently, not pig values or total amino acids. Crude protein includes nitrogen-equivalent contributions from the specified crystalline amino acids and choline; those supplement values were calculated from stated purity and molecular nitrogen content. AMEn conservatively excludes energy from isolated amino acids and unknown premix carrier. Premix macro-minerals are not silently certified as zero: this is an explicit assumption requiring confirmation.

The attached Python calculation checks exact 50 kg sums, non-negative quantities, selected nutrient minima, mineral ranges and amino-acid ratios. Its JSON contains the full assumed matrix, original recipes and outputs. Passing arithmetic checks does not verify an ingredient shipment or animal performance.
